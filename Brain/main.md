Think of it like this — imagine you hire a new employee (that's the AI agent) who's smart and eager, but has no rulebook. Right now, most companies either:

- **Trust the employee blindly** — "sure, go ahead and process that refund, update that record, do whatever" — and hope nothing goes wrong, or
- **Chain them to a desk with rigid rules written into the software itself** — which breaks the moment a new situation comes up that the rules didn't anticipate.

Neither is good. And when something _does_ go wrong, nobody can say "wait, why did the employee do that?" because there's no record of what they were allowed to do or why they were stopped.

**What we're building is basically two things bolted onto that AI employee:**

1. **A bouncer** — before the AI actually does anything (like issuing a refund), it has to ask the bouncer: "Hey, am I allowed to do this, for this person, right now?" The bouncer checks a clear rulebook (that's Cedar) and says yes or no — with a reason.

2. **A CCTV camera + logbook** — every single time the AI tries to do something, whether it's allowed or blocked, it gets written down permanently (that's OpenSearch). So later, anyone can look back and ask "show me everything this AI did today" or "why was it stopped from doing that one thing?"

**Why this matters:** companies are rushing to let AI agents actually _do_ things now, not just answer questions. But almost nobody has built this bouncer + logbook combo yet. Without it, letting an AI take real actions is risky — no control, no proof, no accountability. That's the actual gap we're filling.

It's basically: **don't just let the AI act — make it ask permission first, and write everything down.**
